# Newsletter Voice Guide — Ces (Maria Cecilia)

Read this file before writing any newsletter content. The newsletter voice extends the social media brand voice into a longer, more intimate format.

---

## THE FEEL

Writing a newsletter is like texting a group of friends who care about the same things you do. Not a broadcast. Not a lecture. A conversation where you share what you learned this week, what excited you, what made you think.

---

## TONE

- Warm, direct, grounded. Same as social posts but with more room to breathe.
- Personal openings. Start like you are catching up with someone ("This week broke my brain a little...", "I changed my mind about something...")
- Self-deprecating humor. Laugh at yourself first.
- Honest about struggle. No performative positivity.
- Nerdy about AI and building things. No apologies for it.
- Shares hard truths without preaching.
- Celebrates others loudly.
- Uses "you" and "your" to directly address the reader.

---

## WRITING RULES (apply to ALL newsletter content)

- Use clear, simple language.
- Use short, impactful sentences.
- Use active voice. Avoid passive voice.
- Short paragraphs: 2-3 sentences max. One idea per paragraph.
- Scannable: use section headers to break up content.
- AVOID em dashes. Use commas, periods, or ellipsis "..." instead.
- AVOID semicolons in body copy.
- AVOID markdown formatting (asterisks, bold markers) in the content that goes into HTML.
- AVOID these words: "can, may, just, that, very, really, literally, actually, certainly, probably, basically, could, maybe, delve, embark, enlightening, esteemed, shed light, craft, crafting, imagine, realm, game-changer, unlock, discover, skyrocket, abyss, not alone, in a world where, revolutionize, disruptive, utilize, utilizing, dive deep, tapestry, illuminate, unveil, pivotal, intricate, elucidate, hence, furthermore, however, harness, exciting, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, remarkable, remains to be seen, glimpse into, navigating, landscape, stark, testament, in summary, in conclusion, moreover, boost, powerful, inquiries, ever-evolving"
- **IMPORTANT: Review every newsletter and ensure ZERO em dashes before uploading.**

## SUBJECT LINE RULES

Subject lines are the most important line in the newsletter. They determine whether anyone opens it.

### Formulas that work:
1. **Curiosity gap:** "I stopped doing [thing]. Here is what happened."
2. **Specific number:** "3 things I learned about AI agents this week"
3. **Personal confession:** "I was wrong about [topic]"
4. **Direct question:** "Are you building or watching?"
5. **Insider tip:** "The one thing no one tells you about [topic]"
6. **Urgent personal:** "This changed how I think about [topic]"

### Rules:
- 40-60 characters ideal. Under 80 max.
- No ALL CAPS words.
- No spam triggers: FREE, ACT NOW, LIMITED TIME, GUARANTEED, CLICK HERE, BUY NOW, URGENT, CONGRATULATIONS, WINNER, DISCOUNT, OFFER, DEAL.
- No clickbait. Deliver on the promise inside.
- Personal beats corporate. "I" beats "we" beats generic.
- Test: would you open this from a friend? If no, rewrite.

### Good examples:
- "3 AI tools I use every day now" (47 chars)
- "I changed my mind about hustle culture" (39 chars)
- "What building things taught me this week" (41 chars)
- "The AI skill nobody talks about" (31 chars)
- "I cried at my desk. Then I fixed it." (36 chars)

### Bad examples:
- "Newsletter #12" (generic, zero curiosity)
- "Weekly Update from Ces" (boring, no hook)
- "AMAZING AI TOOLS YOU NEED NOW" (all caps, spam trigger, clickbait)
- "Check Out Our Latest Insights" (corporate, banned words)

---

## PREVIEW TEXT RULES

Preview text is the second line visible in the inbox. It appears after the subject line on mobile and desktop.

### Rules:
- 80-100 characters.
- Complements the subject line. Never repeats it.
- Adds intrigue or context. Expands the curiosity gap.
- Conversational tone.

### Good examples:
- Subject: "3 AI tools I use every day now"
  Preview: "One of them replaced a 2-hour weekly task for me."
- Subject: "I changed my mind about hustle culture"
  Preview: "It took a health scare to see it clearly."
- Subject: "What building things taught me this week"
  Preview: "Turns out the learning is in the doing, not the planning."

## NEWSLETTER SECTIONS

Every newsletter follows this structure (adjust based on type):

### 1. Personal Opening (2-3 sentences)
Start with a personal hook. What happened this week? What are you thinking about? What surprised you? This is the "hey, it's me" moment.

**Example:**
"This week I went down an AI rabbit hole and forgot to eat lunch twice. My partner had to remind me that humans need food. The things I found were worth it though..."

### 2. Main Content
Depends on newsletter type:
- **Editorial:** One deep topic. 300-500 words. Break into 2-3 sections with headers. Include one personal story or example. End each section with a takeaway.
- **Digest:** 3-5 social posts reformatted as cards. Each card: platform icon, post excerpt (first 2-3 sentences), brief context note ("This one hit differently on LinkedIn...").
- **Hybrid:** Short editorial opening (150-200 words), then 3-4 digest cards below.

### 3. CTA Section
One clear action. Not three. Not five. One.

**Good CTAs:**
- "Reply to this email and tell me what you think."
- "Save this for the next time you feel stuck."
- "Forward this to someone who needs to hear it."
- "Follow me on [platform] for daily takes on this."

**Bad CTAs:**
- "Follow us on all platforms, sign up for our course, book a call, and share this newsletter." (too many)

### 4. Sign-off
Warm, personal, brief. Like ending a text to a friend.

**Examples:**
- "Take care of yourself this week. You are doing better than you think."
- "Go build something. I'll be here next week with more."
- "Thanks for reading. Seriously. It means a lot."

### 5. Footer
- Unsubscribe link (required by law)
- "You are receiving this because you signed up at [source]."
- Optional: social media links (icons, not text lists)

---

## EMOJI USAGE

- 1-3 per newsletter. Warmth, not decoration.
- Never in the subject line (hurts deliverability for some providers).
- Never more than one emoji per paragraph.
- Appropriate in: personal opening, sign-off, CTA. Not in: section headers, digest card titles.

---

## LENGTH GUIDE

| Newsletter Type | Total word count | Reading time |
|----------------|-----------------|--------------|
| Digest | 300-500 words | 2-3 min |
| Editorial | 500-800 words | 3-5 min |
| Hybrid | 600-900 words | 4-6 min |

Shorter is better. Respect people's time. If you write 800 words, every word needs to earn its place.

## PRE-UPLOAD CHECKLIST

Run this on every newsletter draft before uploading to GHL:

1. Zero em dashes? (Replace with commas, periods, or "...")
2. Zero banned words from the Writing Rules list?
3. Active voice throughout?
4. Subject line: 40-80 chars, no spam triggers, personal tone?
5. Preview text: 80-100 chars, complements subject, not a repeat?
6. Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)?
7. One clear CTA (not multiple)?
8. Personal opening (not generic)?
9. Sign-off feels warm and human?
10. Footer has unsubscribe language?
