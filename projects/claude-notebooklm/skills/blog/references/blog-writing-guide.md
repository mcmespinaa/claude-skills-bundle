## 1. Blog Post Structure

Every blog post follows this arc:

```
1. Hook intro          — 2-3 sentences, problem or opportunity
2. Context             — why this matters now, who it's for
3. Main sections       — 3-5 H2 sections, each covering a subtopic
4. Actionable takeaway — bullets or numbered steps the reader can use today
5. Conclusion + CTA    — summary + one clear next step
```

The hook should work as a standalone paragraph. Readers decide in the first 3 seconds whether to keep reading.

## 2. Title & Hook Formulas

**Title patterns that perform well:**

| Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Number + outcome | "7 Ways to Reduce Customer Churn Without Discounts" |
| How-to + specific result | "How to Automate Client Onboarding in Under 30 Minutes" |
| Question | "Is Your CRM Actually Losing You Customers?" |
| Contrarian | "Why Most Marketing Automation Fails (And What to Do Instead)" |
| Guide/resource | "The Complete Guide to AI-Powered Customer Service" |

**Title rules:**
- 50-60 characters (fits in search results without truncation)
- Include the primary keyword naturally
- No clickbait — deliver on the promise in the body
- No ALL CAPS words

**Hook patterns:**
- **Pain point opener:** "Every [role] has faced [problem]. Here's how to fix it."
- **Stat opener:** "[Surprising stat]. That means [implication]."
- **Story opener:** "Last month, we [experience]. It changed how we think about [topic]."
- **Direct opener:** "If you [situation], this post is for you."

## 3. Body Writing Rules

- **One idea per paragraph.** If a paragraph covers two ideas, split it.
- **Use subheadings (H2/H3) every 200-300 words.** Readers scan before they read. Subheadings should tell a story on their own.
- **Write in active voice.** "We tested 5 tools" not "5 tools were tested by us."
- **Use "you" and "we."** Direct address builds connection.
- **Lead each section with the key point.** Don't bury the insight at the end of a paragraph.
- **Include specific examples.** Generic advice is forgettable. "Use automation" is weak. "Set up a 3-email welcome sequence triggered by form submission" is actionable.
- **No filler phrases.** Cut: "it's important to note that", "in today's fast-paced world", "needless to say", "at the end of the day".

**Follow brand-voice.md for:**
- Banned words
- No em dashes (use commas, periods, or "...")
- Tone and personality

## 4. Formatting for Readability

- **Short paragraphs.** 2-4 sentences max. Walls of text kill engagement.
- **Bullet points** for lists of 3+ items.
- **Numbered lists** for sequential steps.
- **Bold key phrases** sparingly — only the core takeaway in each section.
- **Blockquotes** for expert quotes or callouts.
- **Internal links** to related posts or resources (if known).
- **External links** open in new tab with `rel="noopener"`.

## 5. Content Length Guidelines

| Content Type | Word Count | When to Use |
|-------------|-----------|-------------|
| Quick tips / news | 600-800 | Time-sensitive topics, simple how-tos |
| Standard blog post | 1,000-1,500 | Most topics, tutorials, opinion pieces |
| In-depth guide | 1,500-2,500 | Pillar content, comprehensive resources |
| Ultimate guide | 2,500+ | SEO-focused cornerstone content |

Default to **1,000-1,500 words** unless the user specifies otherwise or the topic demands more depth.

## 6. Call-to-Action Patterns

Every blog post ends with one clear CTA. Match the CTA to the reader's intent:

| Reader Intent | CTA Pattern |
|--------------|-------------|
| Learning | "Want to go deeper? Read our [related guide]." |
| Problem-solving | "Ready to fix this? [Book a call / Try the tool]." |
| Exploring | "Have questions? Drop them in the comments." |
| Decision-making | "See how [brand] can help — [link to product/service]." |

Keep CTAs conversational, not salesy. One CTA per post.

## 7. Repurposing Content for Blog

When the source is a NotebookLM report, Obsidian note, or external article:

1. **Extract the core insight.** What's the one thing a reader should walk away with?
2. **Restructure for blog format.** Reports are comprehensive. Blog posts are focused. Pick 3-5 key points from a longer report.
3. **Add context and examples.** Raw data needs narrative. Explain why each point matters.
4. **Write a fresh intro and conclusion.** Don't copy the report's introduction — blog readers need a different hook.
5. **Never copy-paste from URLs.** When repurposing external content, write an original post inspired by the source. Cite the source if appropriate.
