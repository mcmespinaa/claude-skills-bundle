# Slide Types Reference

Taxonomy of 15 slide types, 5 opening hooks, and 5 closing techniques. Read this at Step 3 (Build Outline) to select the right slide type for each position in the deck.

---

## Slide Type Taxonomy

### 1. Title Slide
- **Purpose:** First impression. Sets tone and expectations
- **Elements:** Presentation title, subtitle or tagline, speaker name, date, optional logo
- **When:** Always slide 1
- **Canva description:** "Title slide with presentation name and speaker details"
- **Rules:** Title should be a statement, not a label. Max 10 words

### 2. Agenda / Table of Contents
- **Purpose:** Roadmap for the audience. Sets expectations for the journey
- **Elements:** 3-5 numbered sections or topics, optional time estimates
- **When:** Slide 2 for talks over 15 minutes. Skip for short pitches
- **Canva description:** "Agenda slide showing 3 to 5 key sections"
- **Rules:** Max 5 items. Use parallel phrasing. Do not number beyond 5

### 3. Section Divider
- **Purpose:** Signal a major transition between topics
- **Elements:** Section title (large), optional section number, optional brief descriptor
- **When:** Between major sections. Helps audience mentally reset
- **Canva description:** "Section divider with bold section title"
- **Rules:** Minimal content. The whitespace IS the message: "We are shifting gears"

### 4. Content Slide
- **Purpose:** Deliver a single key point with supporting detail
- **Elements:** Assertion headline (sentence), one visual OR 3-6 short points, optional callout
- **When:** The majority of any deck. Core value delivery
- **Canva description:** "Key point with supporting visual or brief explanation"
- **Rules:** One idea only. Headline is a claim, not a topic label

### 5. Data / Chart Slide
- **Purpose:** Present quantitative evidence supporting an assertion
- **Elements:** Assertion headline stating the insight, single chart (bar, line, pie, scatter), 1-2 annotations
- **When:** After making a claim that needs data backing
- **Canva description:** "Data visualization supporting a key insight"
- **Rules:** One chart per slide. Label directly, minimize legends. Headline tells the audience WHAT to conclude

### 6. Comparison Slide
- **Purpose:** Show contrast between two or three options, states, or approaches
- **Elements:** 2-3 columns with parallel structure, labels, optional icons
- **When:** Before/after, old vs. new, your solution vs. alternatives, pros/cons
- **Canva description:** "Side by side comparison of two approaches"
- **Rules:** Parallel structure across columns. Same number of points per column. Visual weight should favor the preferred option

### 7. Quote Slide
- **Purpose:** Add authority, credibility, or emotional weight
- **Elements:** Large quote text (2-3 sentences max), attribution (name, title), optional photo
- **When:** To break rhythm, add credibility, or create an emotional beat. Max 1-2 per deck
- **Canva description:** "Impactful quote with attribution"
- **Rules:** Short quotes only. If the quote needs more than 3 sentences, paraphrase. Speaker photo adds trust

### 8. Timeline Slide
- **Purpose:** Show sequence, progression, or milestones
- **Elements:** Horizontal or vertical timeline, 4-7 milestones, dates/labels, optional icons
- **When:** History, roadmap, project phases, implementation plan
- **Canva description:** "Timeline showing key milestones or phases"
- **Rules:** Max 7 milestones. Highlight the current position. Left-to-right or top-to-bottom flow

### 9. Process / Flow Slide
- **Purpose:** Explain how something works step by step
- **Elements:** 3-5 numbered steps, arrows or connectors, optional icons per step
- **When:** Methodology, workflow, "how it works", implementation steps
- **Canva description:** "Step by step process with 3 to 5 stages"
- **Rules:** Max 5 steps. Each step is 3-5 words. Arrows show direction. Linear or circular flow

### 10. Big Number / Stat Slide
- **Purpose:** Create impact with a single powerful data point
- **Elements:** One large number (filling 40%+ of slide), short context sentence below
- **When:** Opening hooks, key evidence, "wow" moments, transitions
- **Canva description:** "Large impactful statistic with brief context"
- **Rules:** ONE number only. The number should be surprising or counterintuitive. Context sentence explains why it matters

### 11. Full-Bleed Image
- **Purpose:** Create emotional pause, illustrate a concept, or serve as a S.T.A.R. moment
- **Elements:** Edge-to-edge photograph or illustration, optional text overlay (max 5 words)
- **When:** Transitions, emotional beats, story moments, "breathing" slides
- **Canva description:** "Full screen image creating an emotional moment"
- **Rules:** Image must be high quality (min 1920x1080). Text overlay only if essential. The image IS the message

### 12. Summary / Recap Slide
- **Purpose:** Reinforce key takeaways before the close
- **Elements:** 3-5 key points restated as short phrases, optional numbering
- **When:** Before Q&A or before the closing CTA. Helps audience remember
- **Canva description:** "Summary of 3 to 5 key takeaways"
- **Rules:** Max 5 points. Each point is ONE sentence. These should echo the assertion headlines from earlier slides

### 13. CTA / Closing Slide
- **Purpose:** Drive specific action from the audience
- **Elements:** One clear call to action, contact method (QR code, URL, email), optional urgency element
- **When:** Last content slide (before optional Q&A or thank you)
- **Canva description:** "Call to action with next steps and contact info"
- **Rules:** ONE action only. Framed as audience benefit ("Get your free assessment") not your ask ("Sign up for our service"). Include a tangible contact method

### 14. Q&A Slide
- **Purpose:** Signal the discussion period
- **Elements:** Simple prompt ("Questions?", "Let's discuss"), optional contact info
- **When:** After CTA or after Summary. Some speakers prefer Q&A BEFORE the close (bookend technique)
- **Canva description:** "Clean Q and A prompt slide"
- **Rules:** Minimal design. Do NOT end on this slide. Use the bookend close: take Q&A, then deliver your prepared closing

### 15. Appendix Slide
- **Purpose:** Backup detail for Q&A, not presented
- **Elements:** Dense data, methodology, detailed tables, source citations
- **When:** After the closing. Never navigated to during the talk unless a question requires it
- **Canva description:** "Detailed reference slide for appendix"
- **Rules:** These are reference material. They can be denser than main slides. Label clearly: "Appendix: [topic]"

## 5 Opening Hook Types

Use one of these for slide 1 or 2. The audience decides to pay attention within 30 seconds.

### 1. Shocking Statistic
- Lead with one surprising number that reframes the audience's understanding
- The number must be verifiable and relevant to the talk
- Example: "47% of today's Fortune 500 companies will not exist by 2035"
- Pair with: Big Number slide type

### 2. Bold Claim / Contrarian Statement
- Open with a strong opinion that challenges conventional thinking
- Must be defensible (you will spend the deck proving it)
- Example: "Everything your team knows about productivity is wrong"
- Pair with: Content slide type with minimal text

### 3. Question
- Ask a question the audience genuinely wants answered
- Best for interactive sessions and workshops
- Example: "What if your biggest competitor is not who you think it is?"
- Pair with: Content slide type, question as headline

### 4. Story / Anecdote
- Begin with a 30-60 second personal or customer story
- The story must connect to the presentation's core message
- Example: "Last March, a client called me at 11pm..." (then tie to the lesson)
- Pair with: Full-bleed image or minimal Content slide

### 5. Powerful Visual
- Open with a single striking image, no text except the title
- The image provokes curiosity or emotion
- Best for creative, TED-style, or keynote presentations
- Pair with: Full-bleed image slide type

## 5 Closing Techniques

Never end on "Questions?" or "Thank you" alone. Control the final impression.

### 1. Callback / Bookend
- Circle back to the opening hook with new context
- "I opened with that statistic. Now you know how to change it"
- Creates a satisfying narrative arc

### 2. Summary + CTA
- Restate 3 key takeaways, then one clear action
- Most versatile closing, works for any presentation type
- "Three things to remember... and here is your next step"

### 3. Vision Close
- Paint a concrete, attainable future if the audience acts
- Outcome-focused, emotionally resonant
- "Imagine six months from now, your team..." (describe the transformed state)

### 4. Challenge
- Direct, specific challenge to the audience
- Best for motivational or leadership talks
- "This week, I challenge you to..." (one specific action)

### 5. "One More Thing"
- After appearing to conclude, add one final surprise reveal
- Best for product launches, announcements
- "Before I close, there is one more thing..." (the biggest announcement)

### The Bookend Close Protocol
Take Q&A BEFORE your final slide, not after:
1. Say: "I will take a few minutes for questions, then wrap up with a final thought"
2. Handle Q&A
3. Deliver your prepared closing (callback, vision, or challenge)
4. You control the last impression, not a random question

## Slide Position Guide

For a typical 12-slide deck:

| Position | Recommended Types | Purpose |
|----------|-------------------|---------|
| 1 | Title | Identity |
| 2 | Big Number, Content, Full-bleed | Hook |
| 3 | Agenda (if >15 min) or Content | Roadmap or first point |
| 4-5 | Content, Data/Chart | First argument block |
| 6 | Section Divider, Quote, Full-bleed | Transition / breathing room |
| 7-9 | Content, Data/Chart, Comparison | Second argument block |
| 10 | Process/Flow, Timeline | How-to or roadmap |
| 11 | Summary | Reinforce takeaways |
| 12 | CTA | Drive action |
