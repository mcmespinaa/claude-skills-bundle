# Blog Voice Guide

Extends the social media brand voice for long-form blog content. Read alongside SKILL.md before writing any blog post.

---

## Voice Summary

Write like a smart friend explaining something over coffee. Not a textbook. Not a LinkedIn guru. A real person who has done the work and wants to share what they learned.

**Core attributes:**
- Warm and direct. No corporate speak.
- Practical over theoretical. Show, don't lecture.
- Personal. Use real stories and examples.
- Honest about complexity. Don't oversimplify hard topics.
- Respectful of the reader's time. Every paragraph earns its place.

---

## Blog-Specific Rules

### Structure

**Opening (first 100 words):**
- Start with a hook: surprising fact, personal story, bold statement, or direct question
- Never start with "In today's world..." or "Have you ever wondered..."
- Get to the point in the first paragraph. The reader should know what they'll learn.

**Body sections:**
- Use `<h2>` for main sections (3-7 per article)
- Each section has one clear idea
- Lead with the insight, then support it
- Include at least one of: personal example, data point, practical tip
- Use `<h3>` subsections only when a section has 3+ distinct parts

**Closing:**
- Summarize the one thing you want the reader to remember
- Include a clear next step or CTA
- End with warmth, not urgency

### Paragraph Style

- 2-3 sentences per paragraph. Max 4.
- Mix short punchy paragraphs (1 sentence) with medium ones for rhythm.
- Never write walls of text. White space is a feature.
- Start paragraphs with different words. Avoid repetitive openings.

### Sentence Style

- Average sentence length: 10-18 words
- Mix short (5-8 words) with medium (15-22 words) sentences
- Avoid compound sentences with multiple clauses
- Never start three consecutive sentences with the same word
- Use questions to re-engage the reader (1-2 per section max)

## SEO Guidelines

### Title Formulas

Pick one pattern per post:
- **How to [achieve result] without [common pain]**
- **[Number] [things] that [specific benefit]**
- **Why [common belief] is [wrong/incomplete/outdated]**
- **The [adjective] guide to [topic] for [audience]**
- **What [experience] taught me about [lesson]**
- **[Topic]: What nobody tells you about [aspect]**

### Title Rules
- Under 70 chars for meta title (search engines truncate at ~60)
- Under 80 chars for display title
- Include primary keyword naturally
- No clickbait. Deliver on the promise.
- Capitalize first word and proper nouns only (sentence case)

### Meta Description
- 140-160 characters
- Includes the primary keyword
- Reads as a compelling summary, not a keyword dump
- Ends with implicit or explicit benefit
- No em dashes or banned words

### URL Slug
- 3-6 words, lowercase, hyphenated
- Include primary keyword
- Remove stop words (the, a, an, of, in, etc.)
- Examples: `ai-tools-small-business`, `morning-routine-productivity`, `leadership-lessons-crisis`

### Image Alt Text
- Descriptive of what the image shows
- Includes primary keyword naturally (not forced)
- Under 125 characters
- Example: "Infographic showing five AI tools for small business automation"

## What Makes a Good Blog Post

**It answers one question well.** Not five questions superficially.

**It has a clear reader.** Write for one specific person, not "everyone."

**It teaches something usable.** The reader should be able to act on it today.

**It includes a personal lens.** Your experience makes it unique. AI can generate generic advice. Your stories are the differentiator.

**It respects time.** 800 words that change how someone thinks > 3000 words of padding.

## Banned Patterns in Blog Content

Beyond the banned word list in SKILL.md, avoid these blog-specific patterns:

- "Without further ado..."
- "Let me explain..."
- "Before we begin..."
- "As we all know..."
- "It should come as no surprise..."
- "The question is..."
- "Think about it..."
- Starting with dictionary definitions ("According to Merriam-Webster...")
- Ending with "What do you think? Let me know in the comments!"
- Any variation of "subscribe to our newsletter" in the body text
