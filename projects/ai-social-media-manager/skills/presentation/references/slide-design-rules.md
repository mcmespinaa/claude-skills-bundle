# Slide Design Rules

Non-negotiable rules for every presentation. Read this at Step 3 (Build Outline) to enforce quality. These rules apply regardless of framework, audience, or tool.

---

## 10 Non-Negotiable Rules

### 1. One Idea Per Slide
- Every slide communicates exactly ONE assertion
- If you need "and" to describe the slide, split it into two slides
- The headline IS the idea. Everything else on the slide supports it

### 2. The 3-Second Glance Test
- If the audience cannot grasp the point within 3 seconds, the slide fails
- Test: cover the body, read only the headline. Is the message clear?
- One dominant visual, high contrast, no competing elements

### 3. 30pt Font Minimum
- No text below 30pt (28pt absolute floor for footnotes/sources)
- If content does not fit at 30pt, you have too much content
- This forces conciseness better than any word count rule

| Context | Titles | Body | Notes/Sources |
|---------|--------|------|---------------|
| Large venue (100+ seats) | 36-44pt | 28-32pt | 18pt |
| Conference room (20-50) | 32-40pt | 24-28pt | 16pt |
| Small meeting (5-15) | 28-36pt | 20-24pt | 14pt |
| Screen share / virtual | 28-32pt | 24pt | 16pt |
| PDF read-ahead | 24-28pt | 18-20pt | 12-14pt |

### 4. 3-5 Colors Maximum
- Excluding black, white, and grays
- 1 Primary color (titles, key elements)
- 1 Secondary color (subheadings, charts)
- 1 Accent color (emphasis, CTAs, highlights)
- Same color = same meaning throughout the entire deck
- Minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio for text on backgrounds (WCAG AA)

### 5. No Bullet Points (or 6x6 Maximum)
- Default: use visuals, not bullets
- If bullets are unavoidable: max 6 lines, max 6 words per line (36 words total)
- Each bullet should be a parallel construction (all start with verbs, or all are noun phrases)
- Never nest bullets (no sub-bullets)

### 6. Every Chart Has an Assertion Headline
- Bad: "Q1 Revenue by Region"
- Good: "APAC drove 60% of Q1 revenue growth"
- The headline tells the audience WHAT to conclude from the chart
- One chart per slide. One message per chart

### 7. Soft Break Every 10 Minutes
- For talks over 10 minutes, insert a re-engagement moment every 10 minutes
- Types: brief personal story (30-60s), audience question, live demo, video clip, poll, quick exercise
- Plan these into the slide outline. They are not optional for long talks

### 8. Hook Opening, CTA Closing
- Slide 1 or 2 must be a hook (stat, bold claim, question, story, or visual)
- The last content slide must be a clear call to action
- Never end on "Questions?" or "Thank you" alone. End on your message, then take questions

### 9. 25-30% Whitespace Per Slide
- Minimum 0.5 inch margins (0.75 inch preferred for 16:9)
- Never fill more than 70-75% of slide area with content
- Between elements: minimum spacing = half the height of body text
- Whitespace is not wasted space. It is the space that makes content readable

### 10. No 3D Charts, No Chartjunk, No Clip Art
- No 3D bar charts, pie charts, or effects (distorts data perception)
- No gradient fills, drop shadows, or decorative elements on charts
- No clip art or stock illustrations that add no information
- No decorative borders, frames, or ornamental dividers
- Every visual element must earn its place by communicating data or reinforcing the message

## Tufte Data Visualization Principles

When a slide includes data:

1. **Maximize the data-ink ratio.** Remove anything that does not show data: unnecessary gridlines, decorative fills, background colors in chart areas
2. **Label data directly.** Place numbers on or next to data points instead of using legends whenever possible
3. **Start bar chart Y-axis at zero.** Always. No exceptions
4. **Use small multiples.** For comparing multiple datasets, use several simple charts rather than one complex combo chart
5. **One chart, one message.** Each chart should make exactly one point, stated in the headline
6. **Prefer horizontal bar charts** for categorical comparisons (easier to read labels)
7. **Annotate the insight.** Add a callout pointing to the key finding rather than letting the audience search for it

## Mayer's Multimedia Principles (Applied to Slides)

Five principles from cognitive science that improve audience comprehension:

1. **Coherence:** Remove all irrelevant content. Every element on the slide must support the assertion. Decorative images that do not communicate are noise
2. **Signaling:** Highlight key information with bold, color, arrows, or circles. Guide the eye to what matters
3. **Redundancy:** Do NOT display the same text on screen that you say aloud. The slide shows the VISUAL, you provide the NARRATION. Reading slides aloud harms comprehension
4. **Spatial Contiguity:** Place related text and images near each other. Do not put a label at the top and the thing it labels at the bottom
5. **Multimedia:** Use both words AND images together. Words alone or images alone are less effective than the combination

## Slide Dimensions

| Format | Ratio | Pixels | When to use |
|--------|-------|--------|-------------|
| Widescreen (default) | 16:9 | 1920 x 1080 | Most presentations, virtual, modern screens |
| Standard | 4:3 | 1024 x 768 | Legacy projectors, some academic conferences |
| Vertical / Portrait | 9:16 | 1080 x 1920 | Mobile-first, social media repurposing |

Always use **16:9** unless the user specifies otherwise. Canva defaults to presentation format (16:9).

---

## Typography

- **Sans-serif for all text** unless the user's brand requires otherwise
- **Two fonts maximum** in a single deck: one for headlines, one for body
- **Bold for emphasis**, not italics or underline (poor readability at distance)
- **Left-aligned text** for readability. Centered only for titles and short quotes
- **No ALL CAPS for body text.** Acceptable for short labels (3 words max)

For Ces brand: Playfair Display for headlines, DM Sans for body. But Canva will apply its own font pairing based on the selected template/brand kit.
