# Deck Frameworks Reference

Six proven narrative frameworks for structuring presentations. Read this file at Step 2 (Select Framework) to match the user's context to the right structure.

---

## Framework Selection Guide

| Presentation Type | Primary Framework | Alternate | Slides |
|-------------------|-------------------|-----------|--------|
| Investor pitch | Kawasaki 10/20/30 | Raskin 5-Element | 10 |
| Sales deck | Raskin 5-Element | PAS | 8-12 |
| Strategy / consulting | McKinsey Pyramid (SCQA) | - | 10-15 |
| Keynote / conference | Duarte Sparkline | - | 12-15 |
| Scientific / technical | Assertion-Evidence | - | 10-15 |
| Internal update | Pyramid (answer first) | - | 8-12 |
| Product launch | Raskin + "One More Thing" close | - | 10-12 |
| Training / workshop | Segmented Blocks | - | 10-15 |

## 1. McKinsey Pyramid / SCQA

**Origin:** Barbara Minto, The Pyramid Principle
**Best for:** Strategy presentations, consulting decks, executive updates, board meetings

**Core rule:** Lead with the answer first, then support with evidence in a pyramid structure. The audience hears the conclusion before the reasoning.

### SCQA Opening Sequence

| Element | What it does | Slides |
|---------|-------------|--------|
| **S** - Situation | Establish shared context the audience already agrees with | 1 |
| **C** - Complication | What changed, what went wrong, what is at stake | 1 |
| **Q** - Question | The question this raises (often implicit) | 0-1 |
| **A** - Answer | Your recommendation / main assertion | 1 |

### Structural Template (12 slides)

| # | Slide | Type | Content |
|---|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | Title | Title | Presentation title, speaker, date |
| 2 | Situation | Content | Context the audience knows and agrees with |
| 3 | Complication | Content | What changed. The tension point |
| 4 | Answer | Big Number/Content | Your recommendation stated clearly |
| 5 | Argument 1 | Content | First supporting pillar (most important) |
| 6 | Evidence 1 | Data/Chart | Data backing Argument 1 |
| 7 | Argument 2 | Content | Second supporting pillar |
| 8 | Evidence 2 | Data/Chart | Data backing Argument 2 |
| 9 | Argument 3 | Content | Third supporting pillar |
| 10 | Evidence 3 | Data/Chart | Data backing Argument 3 |
| 11 | Implications | Content | What this means going forward |
| 12 | Next Steps | CTA | Specific actions, owners, timeline |

### Ordering Rules
- **MECE:** Arguments must be Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive
- **Order by:** Importance (strongest first), Time (chronological), or Structure (logical grouping)
- Each argument should be expressible as a single sentence

### Example (Ces - AI Strategy)
- S: "Most companies are exploring AI but struggling to move beyond pilots"
- C: "Meanwhile, early adopters are seeing 30-40% efficiency gains"
- Q: "How do we close the gap?"
- A: "Three shifts in how teams adopt AI tools"

## 2. Kawasaki 10/20/30

**Origin:** Guy Kawasaki, venture capitalist
**Best for:** Investor pitches, startup presentations, funding rounds

**Three hard constraints:**
- **10 slides** (no more)
- **20 minutes** (maximum presentation time)
- **30-point font** (minimum, forces conciseness)

### The 10 Required Slides (Exact Order)

| # | Slide | Purpose | Key Element |
|---|-------|---------|-------------|
| 1 | Title | First impression | Company name, tagline (one sentence), speaker, date |
| 2 | Problem/Opportunity | Why this matters | The pain point or market gap, quantified |
| 3 | Value Proposition | Your solution in one sentence | Clear, specific, no jargon |
| 4 | Underlying Magic | Technology or secret sauce | Demo, diagram, or architecture |
| 5 | Business Model | How you make money | Revenue streams, pricing, unit economics |
| 6 | Go-to-Market | How you reach customers | Channels, partnerships, early traction |
| 7 | Competitive Analysis | Market positioning | 2x2 matrix or comparison (never say "no competitors") |
| 8 | Team | Why this team wins | Key people, relevant credentials, gaps acknowledged |
| 9 | Financial Projections | 3-5 year outlook | Revenue, costs, key metrics. Bottom-up, not top-down |
| 10 | Current Status | Where you are now | Milestones hit, timeline, the ask (funding amount + use) |

### Rules
- Never add slides beyond 10. Combine if needed
- If you cannot fit content in 30pt font, you have too much text
- Practice to hit 20 minutes. Budget 2 min per slide
- Appendix slides are allowed but never presented (backup for Q&A only)

## 3. Duarte Sparkline

**Origin:** Nancy Duarte, "Resonate"
**Best for:** Keynotes, conference talks, thought leadership, inspirational presentations

**Core pattern:** Oscillate between "What Is" (current reality) and "What Could Be" (your vision) throughout the presentation. The contrast creates tension and desire for change.

### Sparkline Structure

```
What Is → What Could Be → What Is → What Could Be → ... → S.T.A.R. Moment → New Bliss
```

The oscillation creates a rhythm: ground the audience in reality, lift them to possibility, ground again, lift higher. Each cycle deepens the contrast.

### Structural Template (15 slides)

| # | Slide | Mode | Type | Content |
|---|-------|------|------|---------|
| 1 | Hook | - | Title/Big Number | Attention-grabbing opening (stat, question, or image) |
| 2 | What Is (1) | Reality | Content | Current state the audience recognizes |
| 3 | What Could Be (1) | Vision | Content | First glimpse of the better future |
| 4 | What Is (2) | Reality | Data/Chart | Evidence of the problem, deeper |
| 5 | What Could Be (2) | Vision | Quote/Content | Someone who achieved the vision |
| 6 | Section Break | - | Section Divider | Transition to the core |
| 7 | What Is (3) | Reality | Content | Why current approaches fail |
| 8 | What Could Be (3) | Vision | Content | The principle or framework that works |
| 9 | How | Bridge | Process/Flow | Steps to get from Is to Could Be |
| 10 | What Is (4) | Reality | Content | What happens if you do not change |
| 11 | What Could Be (4) | Vision | Content | What happens when you do |
| 12 | S.T.A.R. Moment | Peak | Full-bleed/Big Number | Something They will Always Remember. The emotional peak |
| 13 | New Bliss | Vision | Content | Concrete, attainable transformed future |
| 14 | Call to Action | CTA | CTA | One specific next step |
| 15 | Close | - | Title | Contact, thank you, or callback to opening |

### The S.T.A.R. Moment
One dramatic, unforgettable moment. Types:
- **Shocking statistic** presented visually
- **Personal story** with emotional weight
- **Live demonstration** that surprises
- **Bold promise** or challenge

### Rules
- Minimum 3 oscillation cycles, maximum 5
- Each "What Could Be" should be more compelling than the last
- The S.T.A.R. moment comes in the final third
- End on "New Bliss" (a concrete future), not on problems

## 4. Raskin 5-Element Sales Deck

**Origin:** Andy Raskin, "The Greatest Sales Deck I've Ever Seen"
**Best for:** Sales decks, product pitches, partnership proposals, product launches

**Critical rule:** Never open by talking about yourself, your company, investors, or product. Open with the world.

### The 5 Elements (Exact Sequence)

| # | Element | Purpose | Slides |
|---|---------|---------|--------|
| 1 | Name the Undeniable Shift | A large change happening in the world (NOT about you) | 1-2 |
| 2 | Show Winners and Losers | This shift creates two groups: adapters thrive, resisters fall behind | 1-2 |
| 3 | Paint the Promised Land | The happy future your solution enables (outcomes, not features) | 1-2 |
| 4 | Position Features as Magic Gifts | Capabilities framed as tools to reach the Promised Land | 2-3 |
| 5 | Show Proof of Delivery | Case studies, metrics, testimonials proving you deliver | 2-3 |

### Structural Template (10 slides)

| # | Slide | Element | Type | Content |
|---|-------|---------|------|---------|
| 1 | Title | - | Title | Company/product, tagline |
| 2 | The Shift | 1 | Big Number/Content | Name the undeniable change (with data) |
| 3 | Stakes | 2 | Comparison | Winners vs. losers in this new world |
| 4 | The Promised Land | 3 | Full-bleed/Content | Vision of the future (outcome-focused) |
| 5 | Magic Gift 1 | 4 | Content | First capability that gets you there |
| 6 | Magic Gift 2 | 4 | Content | Second capability |
| 7 | Magic Gift 3 | 4 | Content | Third capability |
| 8 | Proof 1 | 5 | Quote/Data | Customer case study or metric |
| 9 | Proof 2 | 5 | Quote/Data | Second proof point |
| 10 | Next Steps | - | CTA | Clear ask, timeline, contact |

### Rules
- The "shift" must be undeniable. Not your opinion. A fact the audience already senses
- The Promised Land describes OUTCOMES not FEATURES
- Magic Gifts are CAPABILITIES that enable the Promised Land, framed as gifts
- Proof must be specific: named customers, concrete metrics, quoted testimonials
- For product launches, add an "One More Thing" slide before the CTA

## 5. PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solution)

**Origin:** Copywriting framework, adapted for presentations
**Best for:** Short pitches, problem-focused talks, change management, advocacy

**Core pattern:** Make the audience feel the problem before offering the solution. Without sufficient agitation, the solution feels optional.

### Structural Template (10 slides)

| # | Slide | Phase | Type | Content |
|---|-------|-------|------|---------|
| 1 | Title | - | Title | Talk title, speaker |
| 2 | The Problem | Problem | Big Number/Content | State the issue clearly, with a number |
| 3 | Who it affects | Problem | Content | Make it personal to this audience |
| 4 | The cost of inaction | Agitate | Data/Chart | Quantify the damage |
| 5 | Getting worse | Agitate | Content/Timeline | Show the trend accelerating |
| 6 | Real example | Agitate | Quote/Content | A specific story of the problem in action |
| 7 | The solution | Solution | Content | Your answer, clearly stated |
| 8 | How it works | Solution | Process/Flow | 3-step explanation |
| 9 | Proof it works | Solution | Data/Quote | Evidence, case study, or demo |
| 10 | Call to action | - | CTA | Specific next step tied back to the problem |

### Rules
- Problem phase: 2 slides. State and personalize
- Agitate phase: 3 slides. Deepen the pain. Use emotion, data, and stories
- Solution phase: 3 slides. Only after sufficient agitation
- The CTA should reference the problem: "So you never have to deal with X again"

## 6. Assertion-Evidence

**Origin:** Michael Alley, Penn State University
**Best for:** Scientific talks, technical presentations, data-heavy briefings, academic conferences

**Core rule:** Every slide has a sentence headline (the assertion) and visual evidence (not bullet points).

### Per-Slide Formula

```
HEADLINE: Complete sentence, 8-14 words, stating the slide's key assertion
BODY: One visual (photo, chart, diagram, or video). No bullet points.
```

### Structural Template (12 slides)

| # | Slide | Type | Headline Example | Body |
|---|-------|------|-----------------|------|
| 1 | Title | Title | Talk title as a statement | Speaker, affiliation, date |
| 2 | Motivation | Big Number | "AI adoption has tripled but ROI remains flat" | Single chart showing the gap |
| 3 | Agenda | Agenda | "Three factors explain this disconnect" | Visual roadmap (3 icons/labels) |
| 4 | Factor 1 | Content | "Most teams automate tasks without redesigning workflows" | Diagram: task-level vs workflow-level |
| 5 | Evidence 1 | Data/Chart | "Teams that redesign workflows see 3x the ROI" | Bar chart with annotation |
| 6 | Factor 2 | Content | "Leadership alignment doubles adoption speed" | Before/after comparison visual |
| 7 | Evidence 2 | Data/Chart | "Aligned teams adopt 2.1x faster than siloed teams" | Scatter plot |
| 8 | Factor 3 | Content | "Continuous learning sustains gains beyond the pilot" | Timeline showing pilot vs sustained |
| 9 | Evidence 3 | Data/Chart | "Learning culture correlates with long-term ROI" | Annotated line chart |
| 10 | Synthesis | Summary | "Workflow redesign, leadership, and learning drive AI ROI" | Visual summary of all 3 factors |
| 11 | Implications | Content | "Organizations should invest in people before tools" | 3-step recommendation visual |
| 12 | Close | CTA | Contact or discussion prompt | QR code or URL |

### Rules
- Headlines are ASSERTIONS (claims), not TOPICS (labels)
  - Bad: "AI Adoption Statistics"
  - Good: "AI adoption has tripled but most teams see no ROI"
- Body is VISUAL EVIDENCE, never bullet points
- Max 2 call-outs per slide (annotations on the visual)
- References in 12-14pt at the bottom if needed
- Body text (if any): 18-24pt

## Pacing Guide

| Duration | Slides | Pace | Soft breaks |
|----------|--------|------|-------------|
| 5 min | 5-8 | 40-60s/slide | None |
| 10 min | 8-12 | 50-75s/slide | None |
| 15 min | 12-18 | 50-75s/slide | 1 (at 10 min) |
| 20 min | 10-15 | 80-120s/slide | 1 (at 10 min) |
| 30 min | 20-30 | 60-90s/slide | 2 (at 10, 20 min) |
| 45 min | 30-45 | 60-90s/slide | 3 (at 10, 20, 30 min) |
| 60 min | 30-50 | 75-120s/slide | 4+ (every 10 min) |

Soft break types: brief story, audience question, live demo, video clip (30-90s), poll, exercise.

## Canva Constraints

When building outlines for Canva's `request-outline-review`:
- **topic:** Max 150 characters
- **description per slide:** Max 90 characters. Summarize, do not try to fit full content
- **length:** "short" (1-5 slides) or "balanced" (5-15 slides). Use "balanced" for most frameworks
- **Strip punctuation** from titles and descriptions before passing to `generate-design-structured`
- If a framework needs 15+ slides, split into "balanced" and use `merge-designs` to combine
